## The platform Shane demoed

Two tenants, both on the demo instance.

**Service provider side** (`admin1.demo.prismhr.com/sp`) — Dashboard, Clients,
Workers, Invoices, Admins. The dashboard shows total active workers and clients,
head count trends split EOR / Payroll / Contractors, top clients by worker count,
and worker distribution by type and country on a map. Invoices open to worker-level
lines split Wages / Taxes & Contributions / Fees, with an invoice date and a
payment status.

**Client side** (`app1.demo.prismhr.com`) — New Hire, Onboarding, Team, Invoices,
Time Off, Time Tracking, Reimbursements, Reports, Tools & Resources, Support Cases.
The onboarding queue shows one card per worker with a country flag and a stage rail:
Employee Info → Offer Letter → Agreement → Start Date for EOR, Contract → Start Date
for Contractor+, each card tagged EOR / Payroll / Contractor+. Countries in the
queue during the demo: Uganda, Uzbekistan, Italy, Colombia, Canada–Quebec.

Walked through live: creating a client, the emailed invite that lets the client
enter their own banking information, managers and approval workflows, locations
and how a client groups remote workers by country, reimbursement approval with the
receipt attached, time-off approval, time tracking, and the EOR reports — employer
gross, gross-to-net, general ledger.

The hire flow's first two questions, in Shane's words: "is it a contractor or an
employee?" then "is this an employer of record?" — followed by nationality, country
of work, and whether work authorization or a visa is needed. Shane showed the
employer-cost gauge that tells a client what a hire really costs in that country,
and the local-currency guard ("when you're onboarding someone in India and it's
₹1.5 million, what does that mean?"). Coverage: 175-plus countries, excluding
US-sanctioned ones.

## Their questions, and the answers on tape

**Who does the setup, and who pays for it?** Michelle asked whether Infiniti or
PrismHR builds the client, and what "setup" even means — in Prism today her
department takes the contract from sales and builds the client and the employees.
Shane showed it: clients → create new client → basic information → created, then
email the client so they fill in their own banking. Michelle: "that looks a lot
more streamlined than what we currently do in Prism."

**Do you build the workflows or do we?** Shane: usually the client does, because
they know their locations and how they want to group global employees. Javier
pushed: many of their clients "don't even know how to even login sometimes."
Antaeus: nothing Shane showed is anything PrismHR won't walk them and their client
through, and a fifteen-minute call is not billed.

**How do we make our money back for the time we spend teaching clients?** Javier's
real question, asked twice. Antaeus: the reseller agreement carries a suggested
market price, and the markup is where that time gets built in. Shane: PrismHR
charges a setup fee per employee for getting the employee set up in-country with
an employment contract, and Infiniti can mark that up or charge a separate
implementation fee over and above it.

**Is this an amendment or a separate agreement?** Antaeus: meant as an amendment to
the agreement they already have, and they should take a look for redlines.

**Reseller or referral?** On a reseller agreement PrismHR draws funds from Infiniti
and Infiniti invoices its client. On a referral agreement PrismHR contracts with
the client directly and pulls funds from the client.

**Do we need an RFP template?** Javier asked. Antaeus: no — it is Infiniti going
back to their client with head count and salary requirements. That is what starts a
pricing conversation, and Antaeus said as much: we need high-level head count and
salary to get to a ballpark.

**How fast can we go live?** Rebecca asked what happens before the two weeks.
Shane's answer, the one to reuse: **contractors are immediate** once they are in
the system and have entered their information; **EOR is about two weeks** once the
client agreement is signed and employee information is in; **global payroll is two
to three months**, because in-country payroll is a much bigger build. Antaeus added
that for Infiniti's velocity — Brazil, Germany — most of it looks like EOR.

**How is payroll funded?** Shane: the 10th of the month is the cutoff, approvals
happen against it, and the ACH debit is generally mid to third week of the month —
a week or two ahead, because funds have to reach the country before payday. Payroll
frequency follows country norm: Canada, Mexico, Philippines and Puerto Rico
semi-monthly; most of Europe, South America and Southeast Asia monthly. Off-cycle
runs are possible for a missed bonus or commission, with a fee.

**What if a client bounces?** Shane: PrismHR requires a deposit of one month of
salary, taxes and fees per employee, held in reserve, refundable at the end of
employment. "If that bounces, we're the employer of record in Italy. Italy doesn't
care that you bounce — it's on us." Javier flagged this as a sticky point for
clients used to the US rhythm, and said his clients "need to understand now if
they want somebody in Europe, those are the rules."

**Does Puerto Rico work that way?** Javier, explicitly: no. Puerto Rico runs
through the normal Prism process today — "Tuesday, pay Friday, something like that"
— not the global funding model. Worth carrying into anything written about Puerto
Rico.
