## 1. Employer of record

| # | Most-heard scenario · where it's on tape | How we should respond |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A client wants a specific person in a country where they have no company.** HR Hawaii 9/10 (the Philippines); Infiniti 8/27 (Brazil, Germany, Mexico); Trend 7/20–9/2 (a Manila operation); Regis 9/3 ("most of what partners bring us is employer of record"). | • Say the fork out loud: who legally employs the person decides the product. Nobody employs them yet and the client doesn't want a company there: employer of record.<br>• Type the country on the left the moment it's named. Read the lead lines back.<br>• Ask where the person lives and works day to day, not where they're from.<br>• Ask whether the client has promised a start date, pay or a title. Two weeks runs from the signed agreement and the person's details, not from today.<br>• Don't quote off the description. Send the list, and offer to join the client call. |
| 2 | **People abroad are paid as contractors but work like employees, and the client wants them employed properly.** AdvocatePay 7/7 (Bulgaria, for IP; "borderline their employees"); Simploy 7/9 and 7/21 (two India contractors to reclassify). | • Say the test plainly: who sets their hours and directs their day, do they work for anyone else, is there an end date. The paper doesn't settle it; the country tests the facts.<br>• Employer of record puts it right: an employment contract under that country's law, with the IP language in it.<br>• If they sit inside a local vendor company today, that company's notice terms bind before we can employ them. Ask who owns it.<br>• Contractors and employees run side by side on the same platform. Sort it person by person. |
| 3 | **Someone moved abroad and is still on a US payroll.** Regis 9/3 (their own person, Portugal); Infiniti 8/27 (a client's relocated employee, Germany, referred out); AdvocatePay 7/7 (one in the UK paid to a US bank account). | • Say internal when it's the partner's own person. Same fix either way.<br>• Three questions: still on the US payroll, how long and are they staying, right to work there.<br>• Past a few months, withholding and contributions belong where they live. Paying from a US account doesn't make it domestic.<br>• Moving them to contractor to keep it simple usually makes it worse.<br>• One person is the fastest first deal a partner can do. Offer to run it. |
| 4 | **The client has an entity there and is unwinding it, or doesn't want to keep one.** Infiniti 9/10 (dissolving one entity, opening another); Regis 9/3 (the cost and upkeep of an entity). | • Say the one true thing before the facts are in: opening a company abroad is expensive everywhere and may not be needed at all.<br>• Entity in flux stops the product decision. Find out whether the entity survives; both answers have a product.<br>• If it survives, registered, payroll-registered and banked: payroll. If it goes: employer of record, and nothing has to be built. |
| 5 | **Winning back people already on another provider.** Simploy 9/2 (a client on a standalone EOR for a year, month to month, invoices in hand); Infiniti 9/10 (a past EOR partner whose contact left). | • Ask the term first. Month to month: open now. Fixed: work back from the end date and the notice.<br>• Ask for the invoices or the agreement if the relationship allows. Quote from the number, not against a guess.<br>• Lead with one place for everything: one provider, one invoice, one login, the partner's relationship. A standalone provider can't offer that.<br>• A takeover means the incumbent's notice, a new contract from us, and tenure and leave carried over. Four to eight weeks, most of it their notice. |
| 6 | **Puerto Rico, no entity.** Regis 9/3 (three managing partners, "a deal-breaker"); Infiniti 8/27 (a staffing client that was trying to get established). | • US soil, its own payroll jurisdiction. Say what looks federal and what isn't: Social Security and FUTA go where you'd expect; income tax withholding, the year-end form, unemployment and workers' comp don't.<br>• Employer of record there: two weeks from the person going into the system to the start date; funded ahead of each semi-monthly run, with a one-month reserve. Our own 8/28 summary to a partner says exactly this.<br>• Never speculate about how another PEO is doing it. Ask who files the withholding and where the year-end forms go. |
| 7 | **The start date is set by the tax year or by a promise already made.** Regis 9/3 (January first, "tax restart"); Infiniti 8/27 (two weeks from signature; a person's own notice can stretch it). | • Don't ask why January first. Work back from it: paper and details by mid-December, the incumbent's notice served before that.<br>• A promised date is the deadline. Two weeks runs from the signed agreement and the person's details.<br>• The person's own notice elsewhere is theirs, not ours. Ask if they're working for someone today. |
| 8 | **The client is on the call: IP, benefits, stipends, cost, a vendor team.** AdvocatePay 7/7. | • Speak to the client, land the credit on the partner. Never a price the partner hasn't seen.<br>• IP sits in both documents: the agreement with the partner and the employment contract under local law. Their attorneys can review both; their own IP agreement can be added.<br>• Benefits are paid through employer social charges; nothing is free. Stipends and extras are by country: type it on the left, and if it isn't on file say so and get it to them today.<br>• No at-will, no unlimited PTO, no offer letter. An employment contract.<br>• A team employed by a local company that invoices the client has notice periods to honour first. Ask who owns that company. |
| 9 | **Right to work; nationality versus residence.** AdvocatePay 7/7 (a Bulgarian who lives in Spain); Infiniti 8/27 (an Italian hire, what changes in Belgium; "we can sponsor work permits and visas, it depends on the country"). | • Where they live and work decides the employer, not their passport.<br>• Citizen or resident: straightforward. Visa: check what it allows. Nothing: immigration before payroll; we can help in some countries, and it depends on the country. |

## 2. Global payroll (the client has its own company there)

| # | Most-heard scenario · where it's on tape | How we should respond |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A client already has a company there, with people, and wants the payroll run.** Regis 9/3 (a prospect's entity in Puerto Rico); Trend 7/20 ("is employer of record the only option"); Infiniti 8/27 (two staffing clients with their own accounts in Puerto Rico). | • Three things or it isn't payroll we can run: a registered company, payroll registration, a bank account there. Clients say entity and mean incorporated.<br>• They stay the employer; they keep the filings. We run the calculations and hand the bank what it needs to pay everyone. Their ASO.<br>• Two to three months to live. It's a bigger build than employer of record.<br>• Ask who does the local filings today. When nobody can name who files, usually nobody does. That's the moment to mention employer of record. |
| 2 | **Puerto Rico with an entity, "already paid through Prism", workarounds.** Regis 9/3 (a competing PEO paying Puerto Rico on our own platform; "not correctly, but"); Infiniti 8/27 (live in Puerto Rico with manual tax filing outside the system). | • Running someone on mainland payroll doesn't make them compliant there. The withholding still belongs to Puerto Rico's treasury, year end is a different form, unemployment and workers' comp run through their funds.<br>• Stay on the facts of the jurisdiction. Never discuss another customer.<br>• Puerto Rico doesn't run on the global funding model today; one partner is live on the domestic path with manual filing. Say that plainly. |
| 3 | **"If the client stays the employer, would you do the tax filings?"** Regis 9/3. | • No. On payroll the client keeps the filings and the taxes; we calculate and submit the paperwork to the bank. On employer of record all of that goes away.<br>• Then check how they're paying and classifying today. It's sometimes less compliant than it looks. |
| 4 | **A partner wants to run Canada for its clients, ASO-style.** ESC 7/16–7/22 (funding across the border, T4s, remitting to the agencies, benefits; a separate client needing payroll for a few hundred people). | • We have our own payroll tech in Canada. Canada pays semi-monthly.<br>• Their model breaks at the bank: they can only move money US to US. Walk through how funds reach the country and the agencies before anything else.<br>• A big head count goes to a scoped conversation: head count, salaries, countries, entity status. No RFP. |
| 5 | **The client's entity is in flux.** Infiniti 9/10. | • It can't be quoted until it lands. Say so, and say the true thing: opening another may not be needed. |

## 3. Contractors: contractor management, and Contractor Plus (contractor of record)

| # | Most-heard scenario · where it's on tape | How we should respond |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The client pays people abroad by wire from the US, with whatever contracts they have.** AdvocatePay 7/7 (about twenty-five people across Mexico, South Africa and the Philippines, once Morocco; a wire fee each; "inconsistent contracts"); Infiniti 9/10 (people in Poland paid from the US "all the time, non-compliantly"); a client of Infiniti's paying six contractors in three countries through a payroll tool. | • Contractor management: we onboard them, the contracts are standard, they're paid in local currency through the platform, and the wallet replaces the wires.<br>• Contractors are immediate once they're in the system and have entered their information.<br>• Ask the classification questions before anyone signs anything. |
| 2 | **"Are they really contractors?" Full time, one client, no end date.** AdvocatePay 7/7 ("borderline their employees, it's not project-based"); Simploy 7/21 (control over two India contractors); Infiniti 9/10 (the Poland people "will remain contractors"). | • The country doesn't care what the paper says. Who sets their hours, who directs their day, do they work for anyone else, is there an end date.<br>• Say the consequence plainly: fines and penalties, and the exposure lands on whoever directs the work.<br>• We advise, the client decides. On Contractor Plus we won't sign our name to a contractor agreement for someone who should be an employee.<br>• The fix for those people is employer of record; the genuine contractors stay contractors. Same platform. |
| 3 | **"Do you have a contractor of record?" The compliance worry.** Infiniti 9/10 (a client's new CFO asked for one by name); Simploy 7/21 ("contractor management plus, misclassification protection"). | • Yes. We call it Contractor Plus. We hold the contract with the contractor, we classify them per country before we sign, and the agreement is written for that country.<br>• Say what it solves: the contracting party is us, so the classification exposure sits with the party whose job is to get it right; the paperwork, the payments and the offboarding are ours.<br>• Say what it doesn't: it can't turn an employee into a contractor. Anyone who fails the test gets employer of record.<br>• The fee is higher than plain contractor payments because we carry the classification liability. |
| 4 | **Contractors in a conflict country.** Infiniti 9/10 (Ukraine, "probably a knockout"). | • We price Ukraine. Confirm the specifics for those people before you promise a start date. It isn't a no.<br>• The hard no is the comprehensively sanctioned list, and those countries aren't on the price list at all. |
| 5 | **The partner has never touched contractor payments; the client has lots of vendors.** HR Hawaii 9/10 (single-state, W-2 only). | • It's their 1099 work under a foreign test. Bring the client's contractor payments under the partner's umbrella; it's a piece the partner isn't earning on today. |
| 6 | **Moving contractors off an incumbent platform.** Simploy 9/2 (which product the incumbent delivers, "don't put that in stone yet"); Infiniti's client on a payroll tool. | • Find out what the people actually signed and with whom. The paper decides whether anything has to be served.<br>• Contractors move immediately; there's no in-country registration to wait for.<br>• Get the invoices. They say the product, the head count and the price. |
| 7 | **How the contractor gets paid.** AdvocatePay 7/7; Infiniti 9/10. | • Funds land in the wallet in dollars; they withdraw in the currency they want; a virtual card for online spend. No per-person wire fees. |

## 5. Between us (asked on every call, whatever the product)

| Question · where | How we respond |

## 6. Decisions to take before the triptych (as put, 2026-09-14; settled the same day, see §7)

1. **One contractors door or two.** The record has two tiers: contractor
   payments, where the client holds the contract, and Contractor Plus, where
   we hold it and classify. Recommend one door, "Contractors", with Contractor
   Plus as the compliance lane inside it, because every contractor
   conversation on tape ends up at classification anyway.
2. **Where "Between us" lives.** It is not a product and it is asked on every
   call. Recommend a fixed foot under every product's right panel rather than
   a fifth door, so it is one click away whatever they clicked first.
3. **Puerto Rico.** Not a product, and the single most-heard thing. It rides
   two products. The wing handles the country; the scenario rows above carry
   it under both.
4. **The flyers.** The middle panel is authored from them and waits on the
   re-send. Until then the middle panel would carry the "four, in your
   language" lines the tapes already hold, which is thinner than the ask.
5. **Depth shape.** Product (middle) → scenario (right) → the response's
   bullets with their tape cites, and the list items for every don't-know.
   One column, no labelled sections, per round six.
6. **What's unproven.** Contractor Plus specifics beyond "we hold the contract
   and we classify" (localized agreements, payment mechanics, offboarding) are
   not on tape and are marked for the flyer to settle. Transfer-of-undertakings
   exposure on a European takeover stays ∅.
