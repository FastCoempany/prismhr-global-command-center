# The call corpus — an adversarial pass on how these calls actually run

**Status:** working analysis for founder review. Feeds the Playbook face proposal.
**Date:** 2026-09-11.
**Corpus:** every recorded call the app holds, read end to end.

| Date | Account | Who was on | Shape | Substance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7/7 | AdvocatePay, demoing for their client SubcontractorHub | PEO partner + the end client: CEO, accounting, HR (ten people) | Demo. The CEO redirected it at minute 8. | ~50 min |
| 7/21 | Simploy | COO, alone | Intro (notes only, 15 min) | — |
| 8/27 | Infiniti HR | COO, director of implementation, director of payroll, implementation manager | Second call: read-back, then demo | ~74 min |
| 9/2 | Simploy | COO, alone | Quick follow-up | ~12 min |
| 9/3 | Regis | Two ops leads, a salesperson from her car, the CSM | First call. Redirected at ninety seconds. | ~35 min |
| 9/10 | HR Hawaii | One operator, with our SVP of Sales | First call | ~22 min |

The first scoring was built on 9/3 and 9/10 alone. This pass reads all five
against it and says where it held, where it was wrong, and what the five say
together that two could not.

---

## 1. Where the first read was wrong

**I over-weighted the hijack.** The partner grabbing the wheel in the first two
minutes happened on 9/3 and 7/7. On 8/27, 9/2 and 9/10 the seller ran an orderly
open. Two of five is a real pattern, not the norm. The norm is something else:

**The actual opener is a read-back.** On 8/27 there was literally a slide titled
"What We Heard, and Where You Are Today," read aloud, followed by *"is that a
fair picture?"* — and the answer was *"I think that changed from last time we
talked."* On 9/2: *"you mentioned… is that right?"* → *"Correct, yes. And
actually…"* On 9/10: *"You gave a little context about… want to give a bit
more?"* → *"That's it, yeah."* Three of five calls open by saying back what we
already know and letting them correct it. The correction is where the new
information lives. The face's opener bank had the right instinct and the wrong
emphasis; the read-back is the primary shape.

**I called the four-offering recital the problem. It isn't.** On 8/27 the
recital went out — and landed, because within one breath it was applied to their
own countries: *"for all those countries we're talking about, Germany, Brazil,
Puerto Rico…"* followed by the legal-employer fork. On 9/3 and 9/10 the same
recital went out and floated, because nothing was plugged into it. The recital
is fine. **The recital without their countries and the fork attached in the same
breath is the problem.** That is a sharper claim and it changes what the product
card needs: the country door has to sit on it.

**I under-weighted the partner's own mechanics.** The single most common
question class across five calls is not country nuance and not product features.
It is *how does this work between you and me, and what do I have to do*:

- 7/7 — *"if we wanted to move forward, what would that look like, and how do we
  get that up and running as quickly as possible?"*
- 8/27 — *"The setup, is that something we'd be responsible for?"* · *"how do we
  make back our money for our time?"* (asked twice) · *"is this an amendment or a
  separate agreement?"* · *"do we need an RFP?"* · *"who funds that?"* · *"what
  if clients bounce?"*
- 9/2 — *"What should next steps look like for you?"* · *"what additional
  information would be required to get a quote?"*
- 9/3 — reseller or referral, picked reseller in one sentence · *"would you go
  through with the tax filings?"*
- 9/10 — *"do we hand the client off to you, or am I the middleman?"*

The PEO's real anxiety is their own operational burden and their own
economics. Five of five. The answer bank had three cards on this; it needed
six.

## 2. Where the first read held

**The list is universal.** Four of five calls end with the seller promising a
list of questions for the partner to take to their client. 8/27 names what is
on it: head count, what people are paid, which country, whether an entity
exists. 9/2: *"the questions I'll send you will be pretty much the same for any
client."* It is a fixed artifact, promised at least four times, and never once
handed over on the call. The first read got this right. The list card in the
second-round triptych now carries the two items 8/27 named that the first draft
missed: how many people, and what they are paid.

**The don't-know is the conversation.** Held. 9/2 is the exception and the
proof: one sophisticated COO with the incumbent's invoices in hand, and even she
says *"don't put that in stone yet, let me double check"* on which product the
incumbent is delivering.

**Country depth was missing and the seller paid for it.** Held, and 7/7 is the
worst case in the corpus. The CEO opened with *"I am hyper-focused on
Bulgaria."* Over the next forty minutes the demo lead said, about Bulgaria:
*"off the top of my head, I don't know"* (stipends) · *"I don't know much about
Bulgaria"* (benefits) · *"I forget if Bulgaria… is Bulgaria part of the EU?"* ·
*"I'm not sure what Bulgaria uses. Did they use a euro?"* · *"I don't know about
Bulgaria. Some countries have mandatory registrations."* Five don't-knows on the
one country the buyer cared about, in front of the buyer. Bulgaria's card in the
country sheet answers all five.

## 3. What five calls say that two could not

### 3.1 Countries arrive in clusters

7/7 named six countries. 8/27 named five before the demo started, then Italy and
Belgium during it. 9/10 named five. A country card that handles one country at a
time is half the job; the face has to hold several open and let the operator
move between them without losing any. This argues for the Desk (trail) or the
Thread (stack) over the Slab, which forgets.

**Twenty distinct countries were named across five calls. Nineteen have the
sixteen points on file.** The twentieth is Yemen, named as a joke about
sanctions. The country sheet would have answered every real mention on every
call.

### 3.2 The residence-not-nationality trap recurs

7/7: *"I think one lives in Spain"* — a Bulgarian team member, employed where
they live. 8/27: the demo hire is Italian; Shane walks through what changes if
they work in Belgium. Where the person lives and works, not where they are from,
decides the employer. Two of five calls hit it. It belongs on every EOR card and
in the client list.

### 3.3 The seller's best lines are Shane's, and they are teaching lines

Across 7/7 and 8/27 the demo lead has a small set of lines he uses every time,
each of which corrects a US assumption the partner or client is carrying:

- *"None of this is at-will. It's always an employment contract."*
- *"Unlimited PTO doesn't exist outside the US."*
- *"All that free health care — it's not free. They're paying for it."*
- *"Where they live is where they'd need to be employed."*
- *"The 10,000 a month is really 13,000 from an employer standpoint"* — the
  employer-cost gauge, said with the demo's own figures. The card carries the
  fact as a share of salary, never a figure.

These are answer cards. The partner will repeat them to their client verbatim,
which is the point.

### 3.4 The incumbent is on three of five calls

7/7: *"I think it's called GP"* — Globalization Partners, plus TriNet. 7/21 and
9/2: Simploy has been referring clients to GP with no revenue share, and the
client on 9/2 has been on GP for a year. 9/3: a competing PEO paying Puerto Rico
on our own domestic platform. The tape also shows the right response, from the
9/2 COO who had already done it: find out the term (month-to-month or
multi-year), get the invoices if the relationship allows, price so as not to
underbid or overbid, and lead with consolidation — *"can't this just be in one
place?"* is what her client said. There was no incumbent card. There is now.

### 3.5 Time-to-live is asked on four of five, and the full answer has a caveat

7/7: *"what's the time to set this all up?"* 8/27: *"when could we actually go
live?"* 9/3: the January 1st start. 9/2: *"nobody has a fire right now."* The
answer is stable and on tape: contractors immediate, employer of record about
two weeks from a signed agreement and the person's details, global payroll two
to three months. 8/27 adds the caveat the first draft missed: *"is the employee
currently working for another company? In Italy those notice periods are
long."* The person's own notice can gate the start, and it is theirs, not ours.

### 3.6 Puerto Rico is not what 9/3 implied

On 9/3 Puerto Rico was folded into *"any country, over 175 plus."* On 8/27, a
week earlier, a partner said plainly that they run Puerto Rico through domestic
Prism with manual tax filing, outside the global funding model — *"Tuesday, pay
Friday, something like that"* — and that the global deposit rule does not apply
there. Whether Global offers employer of record in Puerto Rico is still
unproven; what the record does hold is that the domestic path with manual filing
is how at least one partner is live. The Puerto Rico card's open question is
sharpened accordingly.

### 3.7 Sophistication runs the full range

The premise that PEOs are ignorant of this work is true for two or three of five
and false for the others. HR Hawaii is single-state, W-2 only, and could not
tell the staffing arm from Global. Regis knew Puerto Rico was a problem and
reached for their own analogy to understand ours. Simploy's COO had the
incumbent's invoices, knew EOR from contractor, and had already told her own
sales team that pricing is *"very subjective — depends on the product and the
country."* Infiniti's team asked about setup labor, funding cadence, bounced
payments and whether the paper is an amendment. The face has to serve both ends
without patronizing the sophisticated. One card with its depth beneath does
that by construction: the sophisticated skip to the depth.

### 3.8 The partner's fear has two halves

The first read caught one: looking uninformed in front of their own client
(*"not tell them I don't know what I'm talking about,"* 9/3; *"we know what
we're talking about whenever we are talking to groups,"* 9/2). The second is
economic, and 8/27 says it twice: *"how do we make our money back for the time
we spend teaching all our clients?"* The partner's client often *"doesn't even
know how to login."* The ignorance runs down a chain — us, then the partner,
then the client — and the partner pays for the bottom link in time. The tape's
answer: the markup, plus a per-employee setup fee that can itself be marked up
or paired with a separate implementation fee. That is now an answer card.

### 3.9 Their analogy beats ours

9/3: *"like an ASO versus a PEO kind of thing."* The partner supplied it, the
seller accepted it with a caveat, and it did more work than the recital. The PEO
understands global products by mapping them onto the ones they already sell:
global payroll is their ASO, employer of record is their PEO with us as sole
employer rather than co-employer, contractor management is their 1099 work
under a foreign test. Every product card now carries the line in their
language.

### 3.10 A call can be twelve minutes

9/2 ran twelve minutes of substance and covered the incumbent, the contract
term, the invoices, the product, the country, head count, next steps, the list
and the paper. The face has to work at that speed. That is the case for one
card at a time: nothing to scan, nothing to arrange, one keystroke to the
country.

## 4. What this changed in the second-round triptych

- Four answer cards added: the incumbent, the cost of teaching clients, the
  bounced payment and the deposit, and the US-offer correction (no at-will, no
  unlimited PTO, health care is not free).
- The list gained head count and pay, per 8/27's own statement of what pricing
  needs.
- Time-to-live gained the notice-period caveat.
- The Puerto Rico card's open question now carries the 8/27 fact.
- The start card opens on the read-back instruction, and its rows are the five
  things that came up first on five calls, plus the one country three of them
  named.
- Every product card carries the partner's own analogy.

## 5. What it did not change

The structure. One column, one card, the field always one keystroke from any
country. Five calls made the case for it harder, not softer: countries in
clusters, calls as short as twelve minutes, sophistication across the whole
range, and a seller who on the worst day said "I don't know" five times about
the one country in the room.
