The manual, laid along the life of an implementation. Four ways to walk it.

Every version holds the same nineteen chapters and the same seven stages, from before the first call to after the first payment. The first tab is the Lifecycle you picked, kept here for comparison. The other three are new. They differ in how a reader moves through the stages: one stage at a time, the whole thing as one continuous document, a stage at a time as a list of things that must be true before moving on, or a stage at a time as the questions people ask at that point.

The original. A track of seven stages across the top. Pick a stage and every chapter in it opens side by side, in full. One stage on screen at a time.

One document. All seven stages in a single page, read from top to bottom. Each stage starts with a heading and a line saying what happens in it, and its chapters follow as continuous text. The stage list on the left stays in view and marks where you are as you scroll; a click jumps to that stage. Built for reading the whole implementation once, start to finish.

What has to be true. Each stage is defined by the things that must be true before the partner moves to the next one. The stage list on the left shows how many are done. The middle lists them as checkboxes. Clicking one opens the chapter that explains it on the right. The ticks stay in this browser. Built for working out where a partner actually is and what is left.

What comes up when. Every stage on one page, each listing the questions people ask at that point. A click on a question opens the chapter that answers it, right under the question. One question open at a time. Built for answering a partner's question with the context of where they are.
