Ask Russ when a prospect goes quiet

One object on the deal row. It stays quiet while the prospect has been silent for fewer business days than the threshold, then turns red and pulses. Its one action drafts an email, attaches anything from the library, and downloads an Outlook file while saving that same file to the account's record. Nothing here ships until you give a ship order.

  • What the counter counts. Business days in Chicago since our last send, with weekends left out. A substantive reply from the prospect resets it to zero, whether they answered you or answered Russ. A sign-off like "thanks!" does not, under the closer rule. The REPLIED TO YOU and REPLIED TO RUSS buttons in each simulator show the reset.
  • The threshold. Five business days by default. − and + change it for this account, and ↺ restarts the count from today when you know why they are quiet.
  • One press, two results. "Download for Outlook" saves an .eml file to your computer and files the same email on the account's record, with the file backed up under accounts/<Account>/. The receipt says both happened.
  • Who the draft is to. Russ, and only Russ. The Outlook file is addressed to him and carries the note he can send the prospect.
  • Color. Quiet ink at day 0, amber as it nears the threshold, red at the threshold. Red because a prospect going silent is a real risk; orange stays the one move, the download button.

Concept A

A chip at the end of the move

  • Where it sits. On the move row, after Mark it done and the Chute, as a small chip with the count in a dot. It takes no new line on the row.
  • How it reads. The dot fills with the gradient color. At the threshold the chip pulses and opens the draft below the move row. The threshold widget opens from the chip's ⋯.
  • Cost. It is the smallest of the three, so a busy row can hide it until it pulses.

Concept B

A strip of days across the row's top edge

  • Where it sits. A thin bar along the top edge of the row, one segment per business day up to the threshold, plus a line of its own under the move row: "ASK RUSS" with the reason and a Draft email button that stays greyed until the threshold.
  • How it reads. Each silent day lights its segment in the gradient color, so you see how close it is from across the room. At the threshold the strip breathes and the button wakes.

Concept C

A counter that owns the row's corner

  • Where it sits. A column of its own at the row's right edge: the day count as a large number, the threshold beneath it, and the button.
  • How it reads. The number itself takes the gradient color and pulses at the threshold. It is the easiest to read at a glance and the most room it takes.
  • The draft. It opens across the full row, with the letter on the left and the library on the right.
