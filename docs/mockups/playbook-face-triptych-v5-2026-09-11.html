Playbook · face triptych, fifth round · three ideas of a bank · 2026-09-11

Same bank. Same wing. Three different ideas of what a bank is.

Round four’s three concepts looked identical on arrival and differed only two clicks in. These do not. The 85 entries and the country wing are unchanged; what changes is the architecture of finding and reading a response — visible the moment the page opens. A phrasebook, a map, a book.

A phrasebook. Their voice on the left, ours on the right, the whole bank as one dialogue you scan. The find field narrows the phrasebook live as you type what they said. Situations are filters, not shelves. Open a line and its depth unfolds as more dialogue: their follow-ups on the left, our replies on the right, the reasoning and sources under ours. Nothing is a labelled section; everything is an exchange.

A map. Every entry is a tile, and the whole bank fits on one screen. Shelves run down, kinds run across, so you see the shape of every situation at once: what opens it, what we ask, what they say, what we explain, how it closes. Type and the tiles that don’t match fade. Pick a tile and its full depth reads in a pane that stays pinned to the bottom while the map stays above it.

A book. The bank as a numbered manual with every entry fully open. Chapters are shelves, sections are entries, cross-references read §4.3 and jump. A table of contents sits between the wing and the text. Nothing is hidden behind a click; the find field folds non-matching sections to a heading and counts what’s left. As deep as a printed field manual, and searchable.
