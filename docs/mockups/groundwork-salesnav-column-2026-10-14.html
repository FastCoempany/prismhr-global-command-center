Groundwork · the Sales Nav column · three concepts · 2026-10-14

Sales Nav column

Every Sales Navigator alert about a book account, in one column on Groundwork. The alerts lead; the copy-ready note opens as a pop-out. Each concept puts the column in a different place on the page and orders it a different way. Click any alert's buttons to try the pop-out, Worked it and dismiss.

Three concepts · judging, not shipping
  • Sample alerts. Deborah Dhuy at Armhr, Employees at Nextep, Leadership at Engage PEO and Amplify's intent come from your own Sales Nav screens and emails. The two marked SAMPLE are made up to show a post and a departure.
  • No LinkedIn or Salesforce logos: they are trademarks, and their blues are not in the palette. The column wears its own glyph and the mono kicker, in system-intelligence blue.
  • Buttons that leave the app (Post, Profile, InMail, Account, Salesforce) show what they open here; in the app they open the real page in a new tab. The Outlook draft downloads a .eml that opens as an unsent draft, and only when the person is already a contact with an email on file.
Concept A

The fourth wing

  • Inside the instrument. The column joins the same card as the done wing, the stage and the queue, so Groundwork stays one object.
  • Newest first, every alert the same shape: type and date, who, what, account, then the buttons. Filter chips sit at the top.
  • Costs: the widest layout. Below about 1,100px the column drops under the stage.
Concept B

The rail beside

  • Its own card beside Groundwork, blue-topped, sticky as you scroll. The newest alert takes a large spotlight at the top.
  • The rest group by account, so three alerts about Armhr read as one account's week. Click a line to open its buttons.
  • Costs: a second card on the page, and grouping hides the strict time order.
Concept C

The day spine

  • A timeline inside the instrument, under TODAY, YESTERDAY and THIS WEEK, the way the Scratchpaper dates its lines. A glyph per alert type runs down the spine.
  • The newest alert reads large with its buttons showing; the rest show their buttons on hover. One segmented control filters by type.
  • Costs: hover-only buttons need a tap on touch screens.
