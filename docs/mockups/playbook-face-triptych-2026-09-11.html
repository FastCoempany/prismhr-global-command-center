Playbook · face triptych · concepts, not a ship · 2026-09-11

Three ways to shape the Playbook around the call that actually happens.

Read from two partner calls, 2026-09-03 and 2026-09-10. Direction locked in all three: the legal-employer fork leads, the don’t-know routes to a teach instead of stalling, what they ask us gets an answer object, and the relay list leaves the call as something you can hand over. No account or person names, no figures, evidence rungs named on every claim. What differs between the three is the organizing axis.

Decision-first. The surface opens as one question and nothing else. Answer it and the whole face re-lanes to that product line — its gate, its duty split, its asks, its relay list. The bank is never a 113-row list; it is whatever this lane needs next. Every fork and every ask carries “they don’t know,” and that answer flips the surface into teach mode rather than stalling it. Try the gate: answering don’t know to any requirement builds the list for you.

Utterance-first. One input: what did they just say. Type it, or tap a line heard on the tapes. A classifier routes it to one of five moves and renders that card in the seat — they asked us something, they told us a fact, they don’t know, they said something that isn’t right, or they named a country. Misread it? Force the move by hand. The record strip fills on the right; the relay list fills underneath.

Direction-first. Three desks, one running record. Ask is the call sheet with the scenario made mandatory and the fork leading. Answer is the inbound desk, filtering as you type across boundaries, countries, mechanics and objections. Hand off is the relay list as a real object, with the close and a copy control. The three directions are named on the surface so the operator learns the model.
