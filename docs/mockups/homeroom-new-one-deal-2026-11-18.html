One deal at a time

The HomeRoom shows a single deal and gives it the whole screen. Nothing from your other deals competes with it. You switch deals from a short list of names. Three structures of the same idea follow.

  • What arrives. Three things, in this order of weight: the deal's name, its next move with the buttons that act on it, and the latest exchange or the people behind it. Everything else is one click down.
  • The deal's own tools ride its move. When the deal needs Russ, the move says so and carries the counter. When it closes, the move becomes the Judi handoff. Send files and Write live on the move as its buttons.
  • The page-wide tools sit behind one quiet door, Desk, in the room's bar. Write to anyone, Seats and Teams aren't about the deal on screen, so keeping them off the page keeps the deal the only thing you look at.
  • Sizes. Body text is 14px, the move 18px semibold, account names in serif, and small capitals only for times and counts. Buttons are 30px tall and as wide as their words. At half width every size stays the same and the columns stack.
  • Sample data. Real account names from your book with made-up details, labeled where they appear. No figures.

Variation A

The folio

  • A narrow list of deal names stands on the left, always in view. The deal reads top to bottom: name, move, then two columns, what they last said on the left and who they are and where it stands on the right. Trade-off: the list costs a little width, and the email and people share the lower half.

Variation B

The masthead

  • Deal names run across the top like a masthead. The conversation takes the wide left column, and the move with its buttons sits in a card on the right that stays in view as you read. Trade-off: the exchange leads, so you read before you act; a long list of deals scrolls sideways.

Variation C

One at a time, in order

  • No list on screen. The room puts the deal that most needs you first and steps through the rest with ‹ and ›, or J and K on the keyboard. All deals is one click and opens the list. The deal reads as one centered column. Trade-off: the calmest page, but you can't see the other deals' names without opening the list.

Sample data throughout. The email files name their attachments instead of carrying them, and saving to the record is shown by its receipt.
