The HomeRoom, Reformed

Each concept below rebuilds the HomeRoom around the deal facts you listed. Every row shows what the PEO needs, who the client is or that no client has been named, what the client needs and how sure the record is, the countries with their flags, how many employees or contractors, reseller or referral, a folder of every file sent and when, the file sender from Groundwork, and the client's date. UNKNOWN, TODAY and COMPARABLE are gone. Everything else on the HomeRoom today is still here, in a new position.

Three concepts · waiting on your pick · nothing ships without your order
  • Where the facts come from. Each fact is read from the account's record: the emails, calls, notes and files you have filed to that account, including every file dropped into the Chute. Every fact opens with one click to the sentence it came from and the date it was filed. A fact the record has never mentioned says so in plain words, instead of being left blank.
  • What the app already reads today. The reader that runs on every drop already pulls the countries, which of the five products are involved, the headcounts, and whether reseller or referral was discussed. Those four facts need display work, not new reading.
  • What would be new. The reader does not yet pull the client's name, the client's need with a confidence level, or a client's date or deadline as its own fact. It only notes deadlines as free text today. The app also keeps no list of files sent. Send in Outlook would need to record the files it attaches, and the relayed sent copies would need to carry their attachment names. Split between employees and contractors is already in the headcount text and needs a small change to show separately.
  • Colours, and the pulsing light. Red marks a client date 14 days away or less, and its light pulses. Amber marks a date within 30 days, or urgency stated with no date, such as "as soon as we can", and it does not pulse. Navy marks a fact that is lit, such as a product the record names. Blue marks the reader's confidence. Green marks real health: a briefed partner, a closed-won deal, a file already sent. Orange appears once per page, on the top row's Mark it done, as it does today. Flags are the only colours that come from outside the brand palette.
  • A rule this goes past. The brand's motion rules allow one looping pulse per page. In this sample only one date is inside 14 days, so only one light pulses. If two of your deals had dates that close, two lights would pulse at once, which breaks that rule. That needs your call. With reduced motion turned on, the light stays red and stops pulsing.
  • What leaves with TODAY. TODAY held the row's list of open to-dos with their ✓ done, ⏲ delay, ⚑ chase, ✎ edit and ✕ park buttons, the "the record says you owe" suggestions, and the receipts for things you typed into the Drop. The to-dos are still stored and still build the NEXT MOVE line, but the row no longer lists them, so those five buttons have no place on the HomeRoom. The Drop's receipts now appear right under the Drop.
  • What leaves with UNKNOWN and COMPARABLE. The open questions, the stage gate question, ⌕ ask the brain, ⟳ mint sharper asks, → find the answer, and ✕ not this deal all go. The foot legend drops their glyphs.
  • The line renamed from THEIRS. Today's HomeRoom labels one line THEIRS. That line shows a confirmed fact from your weekly Salesforce activity export about one of the account's own people, such as a request they made. The mockup labels it FROM SALESFORCE and writes each fact as a plain sentence. Clicking it still opens the fact and the email it came from.
  • Samples. The account names come from your book. Every fact on the rows is a sample SAMPLE: the clients, people, quotes, countries, counts, dates and files are invented to show the design, and none of it is read from your record. There are no money figures anywhere.
The order the facts appear in, in all three concepts
  1. The client's date, as an alert, because a date that is close is the one thing that changes what you do today.
  2. What the PEO needs: which of the five products, and whether it is for a client or for their own people.
  3. The client: the name, or a clear "not named yet", then what the client needs and how sure the record is.
  4. Where: each country with its flag and its name.
  5. How many: employees and contractors, counted separately.
  6. Reseller or referral, as a lever you can set by hand.
  7. Files: the folder of what was sent and when, and the button that sends more.

Concept A

The facts panel

  • What it is. The rows stay stacked the way they are today, and every row stays open. The right-hand panel, where UNKNOWN, COMPARABLE and TODAY used to sit, becomes a panel of the deal facts, one line each, in the order above.
  • The left column keeps the account line, NEXT MOVE with Mark it done, the stage meter and the latest email, exactly as today.
  • The Salesforce line sits at the top of the panel. The Drop and EARLIER sit under the facts.
  • What it costs. Every row gets taller by the facts panel, so the page is longer and you scroll more to see the whole book. It is the smallest change to how the room works today.
Concept B

The light board

  • What it is. Every account becomes one line of lights, icons and flags, so the whole book can be read top to bottom in a few seconds. The line shows the date light, the account, the products, the client, the countries, the headcount, reseller or referral, how many files were sent, and the date.
  • Click a line and the full row opens beneath it. The facts sit at the top of the open row as larger controls, with NEXT MOVE, the meter, the latest email, the Salesforce line, the Drop and EARLIER below them.
  • The bar above the board sorts the lines and filters them by reseller or referral, by product and by country.
  • What it costs. Rows rest folded to their line instead of open, so the next move and the latest email are one click down for every account except the one that is open. Narrow screens drop some columns from the line.
Concept C

The focus stage

  • What it is. One account at a time fills the page. A rail on the left lists every account with its date light, flags, reseller or referral, and a warning when no client has been named. Click an account in the rail to put it on the stage.
  • The stage leads with a large date banner, then NEXT MOVE with Mark it done, then six large tiles: what they need, the client, where, how many, reseller or referral, and files. The meter, the latest email, the Salesforce line, the Drop and EARLIER sit below the tiles.
  • This is the most visual of the three. Flags and counts are large enough to read from across the room.
  • What it costs. You see one account's detail at a time. Comparing two accounts means clicking between them in the rail.
