The map leads

The room opens on the world your deals touch. A country is the way in, and a deal opens to one calm page of its own. Three structures for the same idea.

  • On arrival, two or three things at most. The map, one plain sentence or list, and a single Desk button. Everything else is one click down.
  • Pins carry the urgency. Red when a client date is 14 days or less away, amber within 30 or when the record says it's urgent with no day, ink otherwise, and a passed date reads PASSED in a faded pin. Pins sit exactly on their country and always above every label.
  • The Desk holds Write, Seats and Teams. None of the three belongs to a country or a deal, so they stay off the map and out of the list until you open the Desk on purpose. That keeps the page about your deals.
  • A deal looks the same in all three. Its name, one line of what it is, the next move with Mark it done and Send files, its people, and only the tools it needs: Ask Russ when it went quiet, the Judi handoff once it's signed.
  • Orange appears only inside an open draft, on Download for Outlook. Every draft downloads a real Outlook file and says where it was saved.

Variation 1

The map over the list

  • Where things live. The map takes the top half. Under it, one quiet list: each deal on one line with its next move and its date. A country filters the list; a deal opens in place, one at a time.
  • Trade-off. The most familiar of the three, and the whole book is visible at once. The map and the list compete a little for the eye.

Variation 2

The map with a country drawer

  • Where things live. The map fills the room with one sentence under it. Choosing a country slides a drawer in from the right with that country's deals; a deal opens inside the drawer, with a way back to the country.
  • Trade-off. The calmest arrival, and the map stays in view while you work a deal. The drawer is narrower than the full page, so a long deal scrolls.

Variation 3

The map first, then the deal

  • Where things live. The room opens as the map alone, with one sentence about what's nearest. Choosing a pin opens its deal as a full page, with a small map at the top to show where you are and the other deals in that country beside it.
  • Trade-off. Each deal gets the whole screen and nothing competes with it. Moving between deals in different countries goes back through the map.
