HomeRoom overhaul · Send · Triptych · 2026-11-17

Write to anyone from the HomeRoom

One tool, three structures. Each one lets you write to a person whether or not they have a row in the HomeRoom, attach anything from the library or your computer, and send through Outlook. Nothing here ships until you give a ship order.

  • What all three share. The To line matches any address against the book. A person on a row says which row. A person with no row says so, and the send still goes; the HomeRoom keeps a record of it for the contact search.
  • The library carries one whole Listo clip. The chapters are not separate files anymore. They are listed inside the clip with their start times, and picking one writes "At 20:12, adding a new employee" into the email so the reader can jump there.
  • The clip would start where Listo starts. The current recording opens on a slide built for one partner, naming their countries and their placements, for about two seconds. Then 16 seconds of PrismHR's payroll menu, then the click into Global Payroll, and Listo loads at 0:26. The whole clip and the first chapter both carry that slide today. The new clip starts at the click, 0:18, so the slide is gone and the viewer sees how they get into Listo.
  • The Judi mark. When the email goes to a deal that is signed or in implementation, a blue line says Judi should see it and why, with one click to copy her. It suggests and never adds her on its own.
  • Sizes. The meter reads the email's encoded size against the 20 MB most mail servers accept. A file from your computer has no 5 MB rule; the meter tells you when the whole email gets too big.
  • Library names. Each item carries its source in mono above the name, LISTO PLATFORM or PRISMHR, and tags for what it shows: PAYROLL, NEW EMPLOYEE, CONTRACTORS, INVOICES. The file name the prospect sees stays "PrismHR Global demo".

Concept A

The desk under the header

  • Where it lives. A Write button beside + add. It opens a desk across the full width of the room, just under the header, and pushes the rows down. The letter is on the left and the library on the right, both in view at once.
  • Good for. One-off emails where you want to see every file before choosing. Cost. The rows leave the screen while you write.

Concept B

The sheet on the right edge

  • Where it lives. The same Write button opens a sheet down the right edge. The rows stay on the left, and clicking a person's name on any row puts them on the To line. The library is a second tab inside the sheet.
  • Good for. Writing while you read a deal's row, and sending to several people one after another. Cost. The letter and the library share one narrow column, so you switch tabs to pick files.

Concept C

Files first

  • Where it lives. A Send files button beside + add opens the library as cards. You pick what to send first, and the letter appears underneath, already holding a line for each file and each chapter you picked.
  • Good for. The common case, where the files are the reason for the email. Cost. An email with no attachment takes one more click, "Write without files".
