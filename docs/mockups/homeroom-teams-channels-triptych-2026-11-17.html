Choose what GEM reads from Teams

One control, three structures. Each one lets you paste a Teams channel link, say why you want it, pause it or drop it, and open what GEM kept from it. Nothing here ships until you give a ship order.

  • What all three share. A pasted link is read on the spot for the channel's name and its team. You pick one reason, tied to your job, and that reason steers what the read keeps from the channel. Pausing keeps the channel on your list and stops new messages; removing it stops them and leaves what was already kept in the Intranet.
  • Every count opens. "4 facts" opens the four facts, each with who said it and a door to its claim in the Intranet. That is one click, by the click-depth law.
  • The sample data is made up. Channel names, people and kept lines show the behavior only.

How the messages reach GEM

  • Option 1, standard connectors only. One scheduled Power Automate flow runs once a day, reads the recent messages of every channel you belong to, and sends them as one digest email to the inbound address the relays already use. GEM keeps the channels on your list and drops the rest on arrival, so the list lives in GEM and you never edit the flow. One digest a day is one Postmark message a day. I believe the standard Teams connector has actions to list your teams and channels and to read a channel's messages, but I have not confirmed that it can read messages on a schedule without a trigger; that needs a test run before anything is built.
  • Option 2, a premium connector. Your Power Automate screenshot says premium capabilities are available in this environment and are paid by the organization's pay-as-you-go Azure subscription. With the premium HTTP connector, a flow could post messages straight to a GEM route and skip Postmark entirely, and it could also read your list from GEM. Every run is billed to the organization, so whether to use it is your call. It asks nothing of IT.
  • One message at a time. Teams' "…" menu on any message can run a flow on that message only. I believe the standard connector's "for a selected message" trigger does this. It suits a one-off from a channel you do not watch.
  • Chats are less certain. Channels are the sure case. I am not sure the standard connector can read 1:1 and group chats on a schedule. A pasted chat link is accepted and marked CHAT · UNTESTED until that is checked.

Concept A

The roster

  • Where it lives. A Teams button beside + add opens a panel under the HomeRoom header. The paste line is at the top and every watched channel is one row below it: the channel, your reason, when its last batch came, what was kept, and pause and remove.
  • Good for. Managing the list. Cost. What was kept is one click down on each row, so a day's news across channels is not in one place.

What came in first

Concept C

One card per channel

  • Where it lives. The panel is a grid with one card per channel. Each card shows its reason, its last batch and its counts, and the counts open in the card. The last card is the add card, which asks two things in turn: the link, then the reason.
  • Good for. Seeing each channel's worth at a glance, and adding one without a crowded form. Cost. A long list makes a tall grid.
