HomeRoom overhaul · Contact search · Triptych · 2026-11-17

Find the last time you spoke

One tool, three structures. Type a name, an address or a company, and the HomeRoom shows the last exchange you had with that person, which account they belong to, and every exchange behind it one click down. People outside the book show up too, and say so. Nothing here ships until you give a ship order.

  • Where the people come from. A contact ledger kept from the three mail relays, the sent copy, the inbox copy and the calendar copy, plus every filed note, call and meeting already on the record. Each relayed message adds its people, its day, which way it went, its channel and its subject. Bodies stay where they are filed today.
  • The relays will be filtered. Once the flows only pass mail tied to your accounts, a person reaches the ledger only through an account-related exchange. Internal all-company mail never arrives, so the search never fills with it.
  • The record already holds enough to start. 103 people are named in the filed record across 30 accounts since July 1, and 11 of them show up on more than one account. The book's roster holds 5,168 contacts across 121 of its 149 accounts. The ledger starts from the record, and the roster only fills in a title or an address the record lacks.
  • One person, one line. The record holds the same person under a name and an address, for example "Chassie Smith" and her simploy.com address. The ledger merges them the way the email reader pairs a name with an address, so a search for either finds one person.
  • Who counts as the last exchange. A message between you and them, either way, or a meeting you both attended. A colleague's message to them shows with the colleague's name, because it tells you who spoke to them last; it never stands in for yours. A calendar acceptance or a decline is machinery and shows only as a meeting answer, never as them writing.
  • Orange is Write to them. It opens the send tool from the send triptych with the person on the To line.

Concept A

The search in the header

  • Where it lives. A search field in the HomeRoom header, between + add and the clock. Typing drops a list under it, one line per person: who, their account, the last exchange and its day. Clicking a line opens the person in the same dropdown, with every exchange.
  • Good for. The quick lookup, from anywhere on the page, without moving the rows. Cost. The dropdown closes when you click away, so you read the history and then go back to work.

Concept B

The people column

  • Where it lives. A standing column on the left of the HomeRoom with everyone you've spoken with, newest exchange first under TODAY, THIS WEEK and EARLIER. The box at its top filters as you type. A person opens in the column itself, and BACK returns to the list.
  • Good for. Seeing who you've been talking to without asking, including people the rows don't show. Three filters narrow it: everyone, people on an account with no HomeRoom row, and people on no account at all. Cost. The rows lose the column's width on every visit.

Concept C

The palette over the room

  • Where it lives. Nothing on the page until you press / or click the small FIND line in the header. A box opens over the room: results on the left, the person on the right, both at once. Arrow keys move through the people, and the right side follows. Escape closes it.
  • Good for. Keyboard speed, and comparing two people's last exchanges by arrowing between them. Cost. The room is covered while it is open, and you have to know the key or the FIND line is there.
