Playbook · face triptych, third round · what’s their situation · 2026-09-11

It starts with their situation. The country lives on the left.

One shell in all three: a prominent left panel that reacts to any country they name — the lead lines, then the sixteen, with a can-we-even line first — and a stage on the right that opens on what’s their situation? and runs that situation to a give-back and a close. The talk track never pivots on the country; the panel carries it. Win-back gets its own situation and its own mechanism: what we know, what we don’t, who finds out, and what’s true regardless. What differs between the three is how the stage moves through a situation.

One question at a time. Pick the situation, then walk it. The stage holds one question, the reason under it, and what they might say. Tap what you heard and the next question arrives in the same spot. A don’t-know goes to the list and comes back. The give-back writes itself from what you tapped; the close is the last card. Try a client is asking right now, type poland on the left.

The whole run sheet. Pick the situation and the entire path unrolls as one document. Numbered sections, each with the question, its reason, and the answers as lines you tick. Branches are written the way a real run sheet writes them — → §4 — and tapping one scrolls you there. Give-back and close at the bottom, the list as an appendix. Nothing ever replaces anything; you read down.

What we know, what we don’t. The win-back mechanism made the stage for every situation. Each situation is a set of facts to establish. The stage shows them as slots — known, unknown, or don’t-know — and the one question that fills the next empty slot. As slots fill, the give-back and the client list write themselves. The four-slot card sits underneath the whole time: know, don’t, who finds out, true regardless.
