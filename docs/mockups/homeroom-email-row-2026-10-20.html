The Email, Opened

The row's left column sits mostly empty under the move, and EARLIER reads as a stack of subject lines with a format code nobody uses. Each concept below puts the newest email into that column and makes every email line in EARLIER open to what the account record history already holds for it: the read's synopsis of the email. They differ in structure: where the email lives, what opens it, and how much the row shows before you click.

Concept A won · ship order 2026-10-20
  • What the app keeps today. The inbox relay files each email as its head line plus the read's synopsis, 600 characters at most. The email itself is dropped after the read. Every line here can open to that synopsis today. Showing the whole email needs one storage change at filing: keep the cleaned email whole on the note (banners, signatures and the quoted trail removed, money redacted), the way call transcripts are already kept. Emails filed before that change open to the synopsis alone and say so.
  • "OL" goes. It is the Outlook format code the readers parse, so the stored head keeps it. The row stops printing it, along with "Today" frozen at filing, the clock-only head, and the "RE: Re: [EXTERNAL]" prefixes. The date and time come from the note itself.
  • Latest email means the newest email on the record in either direction, skipping sign-offs ("thanks!"), calendar acceptances and machinery, by the closer rule. The demo buttons show both cases.
  • Your arrival budget. The build's ratchet holds a HomeRoom row at 39 visible pieces on arrival, and canon says nothing deep surfaces uninvited. All three concepts add the email to the row's arrival, so the build raises that number in the same change: A and B by the whole email, C by a three-line card. Only C keeps the email's body a click down.
  • The other lines in EARLIER (a worked move, a typed note, a call) keep their line and open to their full note body. In C they sit between the email threads in date order and never fold into a thread. The sample shows one: the move you worked on 10/16.
  • EARLIER is drawn open so the lines can be judged; it still rests folded until you click it.
  • Names, subjects, dates and recipients come from the Regis row. Email bodies and synopses are samples SAMPLE and carry no figures.
Concept A

The email, open

  • Your ask, as stated. The newest email sits whole under the meter, filling the column. Reading it takes no click.
  • The email never makes the row taller than the right panel: past that height it fades out, with one door to the rest.
  • EARLIER lines read who, when and the subject. A click opens the line in place to what the account record history shows. One line open at a time.
  • Costs: amends the arrival budget for every row with an email on file. Needs the whole-email store. Older emails open to the synopsis only.
Concept B

The reading pane

  • The left column becomes the row's reading pane. It arrives on the newest email.
  • Click any EARLIER line and that email moves into the pane: the read's synopsis on top, the whole email beneath when it was kept. The line you are reading carries a navy rule.
  • EARLIER stays one line per email and never grows. The pane is where an email opens.
  • Costs: amends the arrival budget like A. Click on the right, read on the left is new to the room. On a narrow screen the pane sits above the panel, so the click scrolls you up to it.
Concept C

The thread, folded

  • The body stays a click down. The newest email arrives as three lines: who and when, the subject, and the read's one-line gist. One click opens the whole email into the column.
  • EARLIER folds by thread. Today six lines repeat "Intro to your Global Business Consultant"; here that is one line with a count. A click opens the thread with every email's synopsis showing, newest first.
  • The same email filed twice (10/15 9:38 AM, twice) folds into one and says so.
  • Costs: the arrival budget still rises by the three-line card. The whole email is one click away, not on arrival. The relay carries no thread id, so threads group by cleaned subject; a renamed subject starts a new thread.
