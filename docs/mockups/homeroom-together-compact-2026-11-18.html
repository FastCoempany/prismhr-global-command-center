The same HomeRoom, using its space on purpose

The same five objects and the same three structures as this morning's triptych, rebuilt on seven principles. Reading text stays 14px. The space comes from padding, labels stacked on their own lines, empty boxes and information said twice. Nothing ships until you give a ship order.

  • What changed from the earlier triptych. Every deal is one line until you open it. The open deal's next move sits on one line with its buttons. The people deck is a strip of names, and a name opens its contact line. Each object leads with one line and folds the rest. The deals share one surface with thin rules between them.
  • How much fits. Measured in the browser with the room filling a 900px-tall window. Today, one opened deal is 610px tall, so about one deal fits. Here an opened deal is 148px and a folded deal is 45px. With one deal open, 15 deals fit in A and B and 14 in C; with all folded, 17 in A and B and 16 in C. The numbers are the same at 1440 and at 960, because the type and the row heights do not change with width. A deal carrying an Ask Russ or Judi line on the row takes one more line, about 45px.
  • Sample data. The deals, people, dates and drafts are samples. The coverage counts are real: 109 of 149 accounts have nothing on the record that involves you.

The seven, and what each does for you

  1. 1Weight and ink, not size. The next move reads first because it is the one bold line in its place, so your eye lands on what to do without a headline-sized sentence pushing everything else off the screen.
  2. 2Controls sized to their words. Buttons are as wide as their label and one line tall, and Mark it done stands out by its green and its place at the end of the move, so it stays the easiest thing to hit.
  3. 3Who and when on one quiet line. A person's address, phone and city sit on one line beside their name, so you read a contact in one glance instead of scanning a column of labels.
  4. 4Gaps group things. Small gaps hold a deal's parts together and a thin rule separates one deal from the next, so you see where each deal begins without a box around every one.
  5. 5Long things stay short until opened, and remember. Deals, the people strip, the Teams channels and the seat batch each open in place, and your browser remembers what you left open, so the room looks the way you left it.
  6. 6Counts are small badges that open their evidence. A number like 109 or 2 ON RECORD takes little room and one click shows what it counts.
  7. 7Same sizes at half width. At 960px the text stays the same size and columns stack instead, so the half-monitor view is as readable as the laptop.
