Hand a signed deal to Judi

One object on a deal row. It stays dark until you mark the deal Closed Won. Then it holds three things: your part, what Judi and the team need to service the PEO and its client, and what is specific to this account's implementation. Nothing here ships until you give a ship order.

  • What lights it. Your ☑ Closed won on the row, or the board's Closed Won. Press "☑ Closed won" on a row below to see it. ↺ on the closed stamp puts it back to dark.
  • Your part. The things only you can do: send the handoff, introduce Judi to the PEO's contact, fill what the record is missing, and the three check-ins the outline gives you to lead, after implementation, before the first payroll and after it. A check-in shows a date only when the record holds one. You tick each item yourself.
  • What Judi needs. The PEO and client contacts, countries, headcount, employees or contractors, the engagement, the start date, the first payroll date, what was promised in the sale, and where support goes. Each fact names where it came from. A fact the record does not hold says so and takes your hand.
  • This account's implementation. What is particular to setting this account up, each with its source: what the reseller invoice can and cannot do today, how a new client gets set up, the sign-in setup, and what the record says about funding.
  • The one move. "Send Judi the handoff" drafts the email to Judi, with Danielle Stansberry as an optional copy and files from the whole library. One press downloads the Outlook file and saves it to the account's record.

Concept A

Three columns on the row

  • Shape. Closed Won turns the dashed strip into a card with three columns: your part, what Judi needs, this account's implementation. The move sits at the foot.
  • Good for. Seeing all three at once on the deal you just closed. Cost. The row gets taller while the card is open.

Concept B

The handoff is the letter

  • Shape. Your part rides as a line of chips across the top. Below it, the handoff email is already written from the record, with each fact in place and its source beside it. A fact the record lacks is an amber blank in the sentence, and filling it fills the letter.
  • Good for. Getting the handoff out fast, because what Judi needs and what she receives are the same thing. Cost. The facts are read as prose, not scanned as a list.

Concept C

One line and a sheet

  • Shape. The row keeps one line, "Hand it to Judi", with how much of your part is left. It opens a sheet on the right edge with three tabs: Your part, What Judi needs, Implementation. The email sits at the sheet's foot on every tab.
  • Good for. Keeping the row short while the deal is in implementation. Cost. The handoff is one click away from the row.

Sample data throughout. Chassie Smith, Simploy's COO, and the India contractors come from the record's July 9 call notes. The invoice, client setup and sign-in lines come from the global project plan's rows 37, 170 and 101 to 103. The engagement, the promise and the funding line are made up to show the behavior.
